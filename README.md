# uber eats promo codes: which ones still clear, how to stack them with cashback, and when Uber One actually pays off

Most people searching for an Uber Eats promo code are stuck in the same loop. They open a coupon site, copy four codes, paste each one into the app, and get "this promotion isn't valid for your account" three times out of four. Then they order anyway and pay full price.

The problem isn't that codes don't exist. It's that the codes with the biggest numbers are almost always locked to a brand-new account, and the codes that work on an account you've had for a year are worth a lot less than the headline suggests. Once you accept that, the whole thing gets easier to plan around — and there's a second layer of savings that most coupon pages never mention, which is cashback on the same order.

Here's what actually holds up, what falls apart, and how to combine the two without accidentally cancelling one out.

## The three kinds of Uber Eats promo code that still work

Promo codes broadly fall into three buckets. Bucket one is a first-order code tied to a specific restaurant or grocery partner. Bucket two is gated behind a student or membership status. Bucket three is a referral invite generated by an existing user. Everything else is mostly noise.

### 1. Partner first-order codes

These are the ones with real money attached, and they come with printed expiry dates. A few examples that have run and what their terms looked like:

- McDonald's US promoted **mcds2026** for $5 off a first order with a $20 minimum, valid through 31 December 2026, one order per customer, code must be applied before completing the order.
- Stop & Shop's grocery launch on the platform used **Launch40** for 40% off a first order up to $25, with a $40 order minimum, expiring 31 May 2026 — a good illustration of how aggressive the discount gets when a retailer is paying to introduce itself.
- Uber Eats' own UK promo page has advertised **£10 off a first order** above a stated minimum, with fees still applying.
- In Japan, the code **UETA4S** took 75% off up to ¥2,000 for first-time users who paid with a non-cash method, valid 1 May to 30 June 2026, one use per person.

Notice the pattern: a hard expiry, a minimum order, first-order-only, and payment-method conditions. If a code you found doesn't have a date attached anywhere, assume it's dead.

### 2. Student and status-gated codes

Student Beans lists two Uber Eats offers in the US and one in Australia. The US offer is **$10 off your first three orders** with a $10 minimum basket for eligible first-time users, and cannot be combined with other offers. The Australian one is **$10 off a first order of $30 or more**, for new eaters only, with a usage cap of 1,000 redemptions — the kind of cap that quietly runs out.

These work, but they're one-shot. You get the discount once and then you're back in the same position as everyone else.

### 3. Referral invites

Every Uber Eats account has a personal invite code, found under your account information. The mechanism is straightforward: someone places their first order with your code, and you get a promo credit that can be applied to your current or next order. The value varies by market — promo-tracking sites report €10 to €15 in France for new accounts on a €20+ first order.

The limitation is baked in. Referral credit is for accounts that haven't ordered yet. If you have an order history, this route is closed to you.

## Why the codes on coupon-aggregator sites mostly misfire

Coupon sites rank by freshness of reporting, not by whether a code will work for you. One tracker currently shows **LOURDES15** for $15 off a first order of $20 or more, last reported working two hours before the page was captured, with 656 logged uses. Same page lists JAMES15, BORDEAUX15, FIVEPOINTS15, ERNIE15 and KNOWS15 with similar terms and "last worked" timestamps ranging from days to weeks old.

Those codes may well work. They're all new-customer codes, which is the single most common reason a valid-looking string gets rejected: the app knows your account isn't new. One promo-focused site puts it bluntly — if your account is more than roughly twelve months old, generic codes stop landing, and the reliable savings shift to referrals, subscriptions and bank partnerships instead.

There's a second trap worth knowing: fake generic strings like UBER20 or UBEREATS10 circulate long after they stopped working, and some coupon sites ask for a phone number before showing the code. There's no version of that exchange where you win.

## The layer nobody writes about: ShopBack cashback on the same order

Cashback is a separate payment from Uber Eats. You click through to Uber Eats from a cashback platform, place the order in that session, and the platform pays you back a fixed amount per qualifying order. ShopBack is the one worth looking at here, and its Uber Eats rate in the US is **up to $4 per order for new Uber Eats customers** and **$0.25 for existing customers**. Tracked in about 2 days, confirmed in about 45 days.

Small numbers, and that's the honest read. $0.25 an order is not going to change your month unless you order a lot. But the flat-dollar structure is why frequency matters: a household ordering three times a week through the platform accumulates roughly $39 a year on the existing-customer rate alone, and more if any of those orders qualify at the higher new-customer or boosted rate.

ShopBack's own food-delivery guide is unusually candid about the failure modes, noting that food delivery has the highest tracking failure rate of any cashback category. The Uber Eats merchant terms spell out the rules that cause most of it:

- The order must be placed **within 2 hours** of clicking through to Uber Eats.
- On iOS, "Limit Ad Tracking" must be off; on Android, "Opt out of Ad Personalisation" must be off.
- You need to clear your cart and close the Uber Eats app before clicking through, then order in that same session. Items already sitting in your cart won't track.
- Adblockers, VPNs and clicking another affiliate link in between can break tracking.
- You have to accept the store's cookies — Uber Eats only confirms a transaction to ShopBack if they're accepted.
- Cashback is calculated on the subtotal **after** discounts are applied, and excludes taxes, service charges, delivery fees and tips.

That last point matters more than it looks. A $15-off code on a $30 order drops your subtotal to $15, and your cashback is calculated on the $15.

Two exclusions do more damage than the rest. **Uber One membership orders don't earn cashback at all.** And cashback doesn't apply to orders placed with vouchers or promo codes that aren't featured on ShopBack.

> Uber Eats' own FAQ is clear on the code limit: you can only apply one promo code to an order, though it can be combined with Uber credits and other promotions.

So the real trade-off is whether your code is on ShopBack's list or not. If it is, you stack both. If it isn't, you're choosing between the discount and the cashback — and $4 rarely beats a first-order code worth $10 to $15.

## The stacking math, worked through

Take a $30 order.

A new-customer code worth $15 drops the subtotal to $15. If that code came from ShopBack's featured list, you'd also earn cashback calculated on the discounted subtotal, so the $4 new-customer rate would be cut proportionally. If the code came from somewhere else, the tracking is void and you get the discount only.

Now take the same $30 order with no code. Subtotal stays $30, and a new customer earn $4 — roughly 13% back, arriving in about 45 days.

Run it once and the code wins. Run it every week for a year on an established account, where codes are largely unavailable and the rate is $0.25, and cashback is the only lever left. That's the actual decision: codes for the first order, cashback for the other fifty.

## Uber One: the subscription that competes with your coupon habit

Uber One has been priced at $9.99 a month or $96 a year since 2023, with a student plan at $4.99 a month for verified students aged 18 and over. New subscribers are typically offered a free trial — reported at 28 days — and you're charged when it ends unless you cancel first.

What you get: $0 delivery fee on eligible orders, up to 10% off eligible orders, and credits back on eligible rides. The benefits only apply at stores and on rides marked with the Uber One icon, and fees and taxes still apply.

Whether it's worth it depends on one number: your delivery fees. Say a typical delivery fee runs $3 to $5 an order. Three orders a month at $4 each is $12 in waived fees against a $9.99 subscription, before the up-to-10% order discount enters the picture. One order a month and you're paying $9.99 to save $4. The break-even sits around two to three orders a month, which is why the plan makes sense for regulars and looks pointless for occasional users.

The catch that links back to everything above: **Uber One membership orders don't earn ShopBack cashback.** Pick one. If you're ordering enough to justify the subscription, take the subscription and stop chasing the $4. If you're ordering once or twice a month, skip Uber One and route each order through cashback instead.

## How to apply a code and keep the cashback, step by step

1. Start at the cashback platform and open the Uber Eats merchant page to see the current rate and the terms in force right now.
2. Check whether the promo code you want to use is listed there. If it isn't, decide whether the discount is worth more than the cashback on that order.
3. Clear your Uber Eats cart. Close the app.
4. Turn off adblockers and VPN, and make sure "Limit Ad Tracking" (iOS) or "Opt out of Ad Personalisation" (Android) is disabled.
5. Click through to Uber Eats from the cashback page, then place the order within 2 hours, in that same session on that same device.
6. Apply the promo code before confirming the order. Uber Eats needs it added before you complete checkout.
7. Accept the cookies on the Uber Eats pages so the transaction can be confirmed back to the cashback platform.
8. Leave the cart alone afterwards — cancelling and reordering is the fastest way to lose both the discount and the reward.

## Mistakes that quietly void a code or the cashback

- Clicking through in the morning and ordering in the evening. The 2-hour window has closed.
- Clicking through on a laptop and then ordering in the phone app. The session doesn't carry across devices reliably.
- Applying a coupon-site code that isn't on the cashback platform's featured list and wondering where the cashback went.
- Using a new-customer code on a long-standing account. It will be rejected, and repeatedly hammering new-account promos across multiple accounts has led to account bans — one deal forum has documented an Uber Eats account being shut after two or three new-user uses on secondary accounts.
- Assuming the discount applies to your total. Cashback and most codes work on the subtotal, and delivery fees, service charges and tips are excluded either way.

## FAQ

**Do Uber Eats promo codes work for existing customers?**
Some do, but the high-value ones generally don't. Coupon sites' best-performing codes are overwhelmingly first-order offers. On an established account your realistic levers are referral credit you send to others, the subscription, and cashback.

**Can I use two Uber Eats promo codes on one order?**
No. One code per order. You can still combine it with Uber credits and other promotions, and you can stack cashback on top if the code is one the cashback platform features.

**Does cashback work if I use a promo code?**
Only if that code is featured on the cashback platform. Codes outside its list typically void tracking.

**How long until the cashback shows up?**
ShopBack's US Uber Eats page lists roughly 2 days to track and roughly 45 days to confirm. The New Zealand page lists about 90 days for confirmation. Confirmed cashback becomes withdrawable balance once it clears.

**Is Uber One cheaper than chasing codes?**
It's a different product. Uber One is $9.99 a month for waived delivery fees and up to 10% off eligible orders, aimed at people ordering several times a month. It replaces cashback rather than stacking with it, since membership orders are excluded.
